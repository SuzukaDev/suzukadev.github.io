Last illustrations I've made were **Pixel Art**, so it has been a while since I made "more traditional art".
That makes me feel the (horrible) feeling that I'm kinda doing a test, and that I have to prove myself that "I still got it" (whatever it is that thing I still want to continue having haha). Art should be fun, but sometimes it can be intimidating to put yourself in front of an empty canvas.

I've been wanting to do a portrait of my beloved Dexter for a long time, and in some way that added some extra pressure to myself; I can not mess with someone I love that much. I need the portrait to fully catch his spirit, I need to fully recognize him. If not, I'll hate the painting (and I'll fail the exam with a 0).

## Trusting the process 🧘‍♀️ <sup>(when the current progress sucks 💩)</sup>

{{< img_caption "trust.jpg" >}}

These are some messages I sent to a friend, basically saying them that "I'm gonna need to *trust the process* a lot if I want it to look good ._.", and the thought that helped me to continue the most:

> "I'm trusting that each stroke I add is going to make it better, even if it is just a bit."


### Short term comparisson 

When I’m riding my bike and I’m about to ride a long slope, a thing that **always** helps me is dividing the route into X smaller milestones. I do not focus on the end, but in the different sub-goals (like, reaching a certain tree, then a rock, etc.). That way, I always feel happy and encouraged when I reach one of those points, and it motivates me enough to tackle the next. My focus is not on the top, but in those little goals. And when I finally realize, I’m already at the very top.


{{< box_info >}}
**Do <u>not</u> compare the current state you have with the final image you are trying to achieve.** Compare the current state as it was 10, 20, etc. strokes ago. This will **boost your motivation**, and make that comparison actually be a good thing, to check your progress (and to analyze the things you are doing good or bad, to fix them).
> Is about **feeling** progress. That sparks motivation.
{{< /box_info >}}

{{< box_green >}}

The good thing is that at early stages, is so easy to feel you are doing it better, since, well, is difficult for it to be worse. The more a painting progress, the more difficult it is to notice some progress, since the details you will be fixing, will start to be smaller and smaller. But at that point, you will have already a good stablished painting (and motivation won't be (normally) a problem 👍).
{{< /box_green >}}


**Is mostly about fooling your brain, at the end.** The final image you are trying to achieve is both part of your motivation and demotivation. It could serve as the initial boost that motivates you to start, but it could also be the reason you could give up with the painting if it grabs most of your focus and its current state does not match your vision.

Once you are in the process, is good to change the mindset/focus, and enjoy the process.

### Little tip
A very simple but useful tip that my brother told me, is to simply painting on a duplicate (or new) layer, and then compare the changes with the previous layer, and decide if to keep or discard the new changes.

Maybe is obvious, and I guess I did it before, but to be honest, not as conscious as I did with this portrait!

Digital makes it so easy to track your progress (in traditional art is also doable, but it requires more effort by taking pictures), and I think is a great advantage.

## The goal is to trust your intuition (and to cultivate an intuition worth trusting)

Something kinda "revelating" for me happened as I was working on this.
I always do all my sketches by eye, I dislike taking measures or using grids. Is not that I'm against anyone who uses them, but for me doing everything by eye is part of the "training". I think it "educates" the eye to be more critical, to notice when things are off, etc.
This always makes the start of a painting the most frustrating part for me. But it's also funny, like solving a puzzle (is funstrating, I guess). 
I need to feel the sketch is, maybe not perfect cause that is impossible, but in a state that is **CLEARLY** recognizable and that catches the "essence" of what I'm drawing/painting. If not, I'm not moving into painting.

At some point I noticed that I messed up the distance between the eyes and the snout. I made the gap bigger in my painting:
{{< img_caption "bad-measure-but-cool.jpg" >}}

Probably there were {{< text_wavy2 "waaaaaaaaay" >}} more things that were not "perfect". But this was for me was the most important one, since the eyes-nose/snout area is always critical. And it got me thinking about changing it or not. 
I ended **not** changing it. And to be honest, I personally recognize more my dog in my final painting than in that picture. Maybe it could be due that I'm more used to see him from above.

I guess that at some point I simply drew him from the image I have of him. And being aware of that, that despite of being *technically wrong* (by comparing the measure with the original image) I was still able to recognize him (even more than in the pic), was a **beautiful and a powerful thing to acknowledge.** It made me realize to not be so hard, strict. That **is ok to push things. And that ultimately, we need to trust our intuition, what feels good.**

At the end, drawing/painting is a very meditative thing. It has some moments where we can stop and analyze, but most of the time, we are just "flowing", solving problems without necessarily thinking logically about how to tackle those issues. I think, deep inside, we are still thinking, but in a lower state of mind that simply does not takes much resources/energy because we are just executing things that are simply "very well integrated in our roots" and we made them almost automatically. Just like when we like or dislike something. We can rationalize that process and think about why we like something or not. But deep inside, we simply know, without needing to put words. 
I think the ultimate goal is to cultivate that intuition, to stay in that state of mind.

{{< box_green >}}
Do not be scared about making mistakes or to distort things, even if you think those things are important (like in my case I thought it was the proportions). It may lead to better results, and will make painting more fun, since you will be less "tied".
{{< /box_green >}}

### How to cultivate an intuition worth trusting
I think this depends of the person, but I personally think that by being critic with what we think and by trying to put words about the thoughts that happens in our head when it's in "automatic mode".

I like writing these blog entries and writing all these thoughts I have while working on something, because at the end they serve me as a link between the things I feel, and the things I think. It helps me to realize that the decissions I take without thinking that much, are supported by a logic.

Personally, I don't think that I'm "good" at drawing or painting, but I know that I'm very critic, and I trust that after every iteration, things will get better and better.

## About brushes

I used to have lots of brushes, from different Krita bundles, and lots made by myself, all of them very well organized. I had brushes for everything, for imitating different mediums, textures, effects. And I felt that my work was kinda “rocketed” by them.

But after loosing them due to problems with my computer (not just once, but many times), I end feeling very discouraged, since I put a lot of work in them. I still had backups of them, but everytime my computer failed, **they felt like a bag I had to carry with myself**, locate them, install again.

At some point, I stopped caring about them completely. 

Now, my relationship with brushes is quite different; **brushes for me are like toys**. Like things that can bring you joy, because they are simply fun to use, and they can spark a bit of motivation for you to paint or draw.

They can help you overcome specific problems, and some brushes may be better suited to certain tasks than others. But ultimately, how good or bad a brush is for a particular purpose usually comes down to just a few factors:
- Opacity
- Brush tip edges (smooth or hard)
- Brush tip shape (not as important as the other two, in my opinion, but sometimes relevant)

That's pretty much it. Nothing more, really. Everything else is optional.

And I think that sometimes, certain brushes can add a little bit of personality to a piece or even become part of an artist's identity.

I think of this square brush that creates a kind of glitchy vibe, with its hue variation and hard contrast, which (the freaking awesome) [Alariko](https://www.artstation.com/alariko) uses a lot in their illustrations:

{{< img_caption "Alariko-brush.png" >}}

> You can see [Alariko's illustrations here](https://www.artstation.com/alariko). (*I don't want to directly post them here without permision*).


I think it adds a cool touch. And since they use it in so many of their illustrations, I love the idea that at some point, someone might recognize the brush and think, “Oh, this painting looks like it could be by Alariko!”

But is just that, a little detail, not very important. Alariko’s work would obviously still as good if it didn’t had that brush, of course. It just adds a little touch.

{{< box_green "💡" >}}
So, if I had to choose between “brushes matter” or “brushes doesn’t matter”, I’d choose this last; **brushes doesn’t matter.** You can achieve anything with basic brushes.

> And I personally think that, rather than having tons of brushes, is way more important to know how to make precise changes to your current brush, depending on what you want. Is way more powerful and faster than having to find the *perfect* brush.
{{< /box_green >}}

## Some thoughts about color

My initial goal with the colors in this painting was to use some "unusual" colors. I simply thought about green, because I noticed some subtle greens in the picture, and I kinda wanted to push them. I literally threw all the colors I wanted, I focused on the values mainly. 
I realized that at some point, by throwing colors like a mad person, the painting ended being like a palette where I literally already had all the colors I needed. And for that reason, was super easy to achive something cohesive without too much thought.

{{< box_green >}}
Throw all the colors you want. Preferably in the early stages of a painting (is always harder to achive verations in final stages).
{{< /box_green >}}

I kept that thought in mind a lot, when I said that I ended considering the colors I already threw in the painting as my palette.
{{< /details >}}

And I recalled [this video by Marco Bucci](https://youtu.be/4LhcNbFMkTw?t=20), and these quotes:

{{< quote "I don't know what I'm mixing. The colours on my palette are like a piano... and I'm just hittin' the keys" "Morgan Weistling" >}} 
{{< quote "I don't look at the colours on my palette. There's a warm side, a cool side, and a few odds and ends in the middle." "Joseph Zbukvic" >}} 

I felt like this when adding the colors. Color theory is always hard, because colors are deeply tied to their context/surrounding. So at some point, yes, is like hitting the keys of a piano to find the note for a melody.

At the end it's as simple as to **judge** a color from its 3 basic "dimensions"; tone, value, and saturation. If we judge the colors by being aware of which element/*dimension* is not working, is always much easier to fine tune to find the color we want. 
{{< box_info >}}
The value is usually the most important one most of the times since it conveys the depth. It could be like the pitch of a note when making a song or a melody. Saturation could be like the volume that a note is played, how much attention do you want it to gather. We don't want all the notes in a melody to scream at our ears. The tone/hue, the timbre of the instrument that plays the note.
{{< /box_info >}}

{{< box_green >}}
Don't be afraid to choose and use any color you want. Once you put it in the canvas, you will know if it works or not (and if not, just by making some changes (changing its value, saturation, etc) will probably make it work). Don't be afraid to paint over, digital paint is free!
{{< /box_green >}}
